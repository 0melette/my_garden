# Radish - Chinese Green Qingluobo

![[localdata/plant_images/42-radish-chinese-green-qingluobo.jpg]]

## Summary

Chinese green radish, also called Qingluobo or green daikon, has elongated roots with green skin and green flesh; the tip may fade to white. Roots are crisp, with sweetness and variable peppery heat. Specialty Produce describes green daikon as relatively mild, while Gardening Know How describes Qingluobo as spicy with a sweet undertone. Expect flavour to vary with cultivar and growing conditions.

## Sowing

Direct sow for a cool-season crop. Grand Prismatic Seed recommends early spring or late summer sowing and thinning to about 10 cm. Gardening Know How favours autumn sowing for Qingluobo because summer heat can encourage bolting. These seasonal recommendations are general guidance, not a verified Sydney month-by-month calendar.

## Care

Leave enough room for the long roots to develop. The spacing property follows Grand Prismatic Seed's 4-inch thinning recommendation, rounded to 10 cm. Other numeric growing requirements remain unverified.

## Management

Watch for premature flowering in hot weather; bolting redirects growth away from the edible root. If buying the treated Qingluobo seed listing supplied by the user, follow the packet's handling instructions.

## Harvest

Gardening Know How reports approximately 60 days from seed to maturity; actual timing depends on cultivar and conditions. Its reported root length is roughly 15–30 cm. The user's ABSeeds excerpt gives 12–25 cm; that product page was not independently retrieved.

## Uses

Slice or grate raw for salads, pickle, or cook. The green flesh adds colour to raw dishes. Gardening Know How also describes the leaves as edible and peppery.
